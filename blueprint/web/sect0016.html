  • PeterWeyl.UnitaryRep.IsIrreducible.intertwiner_self_eq_smul

Suppose \(\rho : \texttt{UnitaryRep G H}\) is irreducible, and let \(T : H \to _L H\) be compact with \(T.\texttt{IsIntertwiner $\rho $ $\rho $}\). Then there exists \(z \in \mathbb C\) with \(T = z \cdot \mathrm{Id}\).

Proof ▶

Apply Theorem 52 to obtain \(\mathrm{Re}(T), \mathrm{Im}(T)\) both self-adjoint and equivariant. Their compactness follows from compactness of \(T\) and \(T^*\) (compact operators are closed under adjoint and linear combinations: Mathlib’s IsCompactOperator.add, IsCompactOperator.smul, IsCompactOperator.adjoint). By Theorem 54, each of \(\mathrm{Re}(T), \mathrm{Im}(T)\) is a real scalar (or zero); their combination \(T = \mathrm{Re}(T) + i\, \mathrm{Im}(T)\) is a complex scalar.

This Schur is weaker than the classical statement (which removes the compactness hypothesis on \(T\)): we use compactness of \(T\) to invoke the spectral theorem (Assumption 49). The full Schur for arbitrary bounded equivariant \(T\) requires the spectral theorem for bounded self-adjoint operators (a bigger black box), but in our application every intertwiner we will encounter is compact (it factors through finite-dimensional or compact-operator endpoints).

  • PeterWeyl.UnitaryRep.IsIrreducible.compact_intertwiner_eq_zero_of_not_equiv

Let \(\rho : \texttt{UnitaryRep G H}\), \(\sigma : \texttt{UnitaryRep G K}\) both irreducible, and \(T : H \to _L K\) compact with \(T.\texttt{IsIntertwiner $\rho $ $\sigma $}\). If \(\rho \not\cong \sigma \) (no unitary equivalence between \(\rho \) and \(\sigma \)), then \(T = 0\).

Proof ▶

Suppose \(T \neq 0\). Since \(\overline{\ker T}\) is closed and \(\rho \)-invariant (intertwiner property gives invariance, then take closure), and \(\rho \) is irreducible, \(\overline{\ker T} \in \{ 0, H\} \). If \(\overline{\ker T} = H\), density gives \(T = 0\), contradiction. So \(\ker T = 0\) (the kernel itself is closed for bounded \(T\) already), i.e. \(T\) is injective.

Similarly \(\overline{T(H)}\) is closed and \(\sigma \)-invariant, so \(\overline{T(H)} \in \{ 0, K\} \) by irreducibility of \(\sigma \). The case \(\overline{T(H)} = 0\) gives \(T = 0\), contradiction; so \(\overline{T(H)} = K\), i.e. \(T\) has dense range.

Consider \(T^* T : H \to _L H\). It is self-adjoint, positive, \(\rho \)-equivariant, and compact (compact operators absorb bounded ones: \(T\) compact \(\Rightarrow \) \(T^*T\) compact). By Theorem 55, \(T^* T = c \cdot \mathrm{Id}\) for some \(c \in \mathbb C\). Positivity and \(T \neq 0\) force \(c {\gt} 0\).

Set \(U := T / \sqrt c : H \to _L K\). Then \(U^* U = \mathrm{Id}_H\), so \(U\) is an isometry. Density of \(T(H)\) in \(K\) implies density of \(U(H)\) in \(K\), hence (since the image of an isometry is closed) \(U(H) = K\). So \(U\) is a unitary equivalence \(\rho \to \sigma \), contradicting \(\rho \not\cong \sigma \).
