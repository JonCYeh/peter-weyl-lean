8.3 From finite-dim invariant to finite-dim irreducible

Theorem 73 Every finite-dim invariant subspace contains an irreducible

  • PeterWeyl.UnitaryRep.exists_irreducible_subspace_of_finiteDim

Let \(W\) be a finite-dim non-zero closed subspace of \(H\) with \(\rho .\texttt{IsInvariant}\, W\). Then there exists a non-zero closed subspace \(W' \subseteq W\) with \(\rho .\texttt{IsInvariant}\, W'\) such that \(\rho |_{W'}\) is irreducible.

Proof ▶

Induction on \(\dim W\). Base case \(\dim W = 1\): \(W\) is itself irreducible. Inductive step: if \(\rho |_W\) has a proper non-zero invariant subspace \(W_1 \subsetneq W\), by Theorem 19 \(W_1^\perp \cap W\) is also \(\rho \)-invariant of strictly smaller dimension. By induction one of the two pieces \(W_1\) or \(W_1^\perp \cap W\) contains an irreducible subspace; that is our \(W'\). Otherwise \(\rho |_W\) has only \(\{ 0\} \) and \(W\) as invariant subspaces, so \(\rho |_W\) itself is irreducible; take \(W' := W\).

  • PeterWeyl.UnitaryRep.exists_irreducible_subspace_acting_nontrivially

Let \(W\) be a finite-dim non-zero closed \(\rho \)-invariant subspace, and \(g_0 \in G\) such that \(\rho (g_0)|_W \neq \mathrm{Id}_W\). Then there exists a non-zero closed \(\rho \)-invariant subspace \(W' \subseteq W\) with \(\rho |_{W'}\) irreducible and \(\rho (g_0)|_{W'} \neq \mathrm{Id}_{W'}\).

Proof ▶

By induction on \(\dim W\), with the same splitting as in Theorem 73, but at each split step pick the side on which \(\rho (g_0)\) still acts non-trivially. (At least one of the two pieces in \(W = W_1 \oplus (W_1^\perp \cap W)\) has \(\rho (g_0)\) non-trivial: if \(\rho (g_0)\) acted as \(\mathrm{Id}\) on both, it would act as \(\mathrm{Id}\) on all of \(W\), contradicting hypothesis.) Continue until reaching an irreducible subspace; since the property of “\(\rho (g_0)\) acts non-trivially” is preserved at each step, the resulting irreducible \(W'\) has \(\rho (g_0)|_{W'} \neq \mathrm{Id}\).
