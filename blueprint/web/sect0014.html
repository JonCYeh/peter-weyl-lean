6.1 Decomposition into self-adjoint pieces

For \(T : H \to _L H\) on a complex Hilbert space, define \(\mathrm{Re}(T) := (T + T^*)/2\) and \(\mathrm{Im}(T) := (T - T^*)/(2i)\), both self-adjoint, with \(T = \mathrm{Re}(T) + i\, \mathrm{Im}(T)\).

If \(T : H \to _L H\) has \(T.\texttt{IsIntertwiner $\rho $ $\rho $}\), then so do \(\mathrm{Re}(T)\) and \(\mathrm{Im}(T)\).

Proof ▶

Equivariance for \(T^*\): from \(T \rho (g) = \rho (g) T\), take adjoints to get \(\rho (g)^* T^* = T^* \rho (g)^*\). By unitarity (Theorem 15), \(\rho (g)^* = \rho (g^{-1})\). So \(T^* \rho (g^{-1}) = \rho (g^{-1}) T^*\) for every \(g\), equivalently \(T^*\) intertwines \(\rho \) with itself. Linear combinations preserve the intertwining property.
