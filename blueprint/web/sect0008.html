2.5 The left and right regular representations

Definition 27 Left translation as a map on \(L^2\)
#

Uses

  • Definition 9
  • Theorem 6

Lean declarations

  • PeterWeyl.leftTransL2

For \(g \in G\), the left translation \(\lambda _g : L^2(G) \to L^2(G)\) is the unique continuous linear map sending the class of \(f \in C(G)\) to the class of \(h \mapsto f(g^{-1} h)\). Existence: on continuous functions left translation preserves the \(L^2\)-norm by left-invariance of \(\mu _G\) (Theorem 6), hence extends uniquely by continuity to all of \(L^2(G)\) (Theorem 12).

Theorem 28 \(\lambda _g\) is unitary
#

Uses

Lean declarations

  • PeterWeyl.leftTransL2_isUnitary

For each \(g \in G\), \(\lambda _g\) is a unitary operator on \(L^2(G)\).

Proof ▶

By construction \(\lambda _g\) preserves \(\| \cdot \| _{L^2}\) on the dense subspace of continuous functions. Continuity extends this to all of \(L^2(G)\). The inverse is \(\lambda _{g^{-1}}\).

Theorem 30 The orbit map of left translation is continuous
#

Uses

  • Theorem 12
  • Definition 27

Lean declarations

  • PeterWeyl.leftTransL2_strongContinuous

For every \(f \in L^2(G)\), the map \(g \mapsto \lambda _g f\) is continuous from \(G\) to \(L^2(G)\).

Proof ▶

Three steps.

Step 1: continuous functions are uniformly continuous on \(G\). Mathlib’s CompactSpace.uniformContinuous_of_continuous applied to \(f \in C(G, \mathbb C)\).

Step 2: convergence on continuous \(f\). For \(f \in C(G)\) and \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\), uniform continuity gives a neighbourhood \(U\) of \(1_G\) with \(|f(u^{-1}h) - f(h)| {\lt} \varepsilon \) for all \(u \in U, h \in G\). Then \(\| \lambda _u f - f\| _{L^2}^2 \leq \varepsilon ^2\) since \(\mu _G(G) = 1\). By group homomorphism (Theorem 29), \(g \mapsto \lambda _g f\) is continuous at every \(g \in G\).

Step 3: extend to all \(f \in L^2\). Let \(f \in L^2(G)\), \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\). Pick \(f' \in C(G)\) with \(\| f - f'\| _{L^2} {\lt} \varepsilon /3\) (Theorem 12). By Step 2, pick a neighbourhood \(U\) of \(g\) in \(G\) with \(\| \lambda _h f' - \lambda _g f'\| _{L^2} {\lt} \varepsilon /3\) for \(h \in U\). Triangle inequality with \(\| \lambda _h\| _{\mathrm{op}} = 1\) (Theorem 28) gives the bound for \(f\).

Lean declarations

  • PeterWeyl.leftReg_comm_rightReg

For all \(g, h \in G\), \(\rho ^L(g) \circ _L \rho ^R(h) = \rho ^R(h) \circ _L \rho ^L(g)\).

Proof ▶

On the dense subspace of continuous functions: \((\rho ^L(g)\rho ^R(h)f)(x) = f(g^{-1} x h) = (\rho ^R(h)\rho ^L(g)f)(x)\), using associativity of the group operation. Continuity extends to all of \(L^2\).
