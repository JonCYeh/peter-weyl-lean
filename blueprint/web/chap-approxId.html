4 Approximate Identities

We need a family of symmetric continuous bumps \(\{ \phi _U\} \) supported in shrinking neighbourhoods of \(1_G\), with \(T_{\phi _U} \to \mathrm{Id}\) strongly as \(U \to \{ 1_G\} \).

  • PeterWeyl.IsBump

Given an open neighbourhood \(U\) of \(1_G\), a function \(\phi : G \to \mathbb R\) is a bump on \(U\) if it is continuous, non-negative, has support contained in \(U\), satisfies \(\int _G \phi \, d\mu _G = 1\), and \(\phi (g) = \phi (g^{-1})\) for all \(g\).

  • PeterWeyl.exists_bump

For every open \(U \ni 1_G\), there exists \(\phi : G \to \mathbb R\) that is a bump on \(U\).

Proof ▶

Use Urysohn’s lemma in the locally compact Hausdorff group \(G\) to obtain a continuous non-negative \(\psi \in C(G)\) with \(\psi (1_G) {\gt} 0\) and \(\mathrm{supp}\, \psi \subseteq U\). Pick a smaller open \(V \ni 1_G\) with \(V \cup V^{-1} \subseteq U\) and shrink \(\psi \)’s support if necessary. Symmetrize: set \(\psi ^*(g) := \psi (g) + \psi (g^{-1})\). Then \(\psi ^*\) has support in \(V \cup V^{-1} \subseteq U\) and is symmetric. Normalise: \(\phi := \psi ^* / \int \psi ^*\, d\mu _G\) (positive denominator because \(\psi ^*(1_G) {\gt} 0\) and \(\psi ^*\) continuous non-negative implies a positive integral on any non-empty open set by the open-positivity of \(\mu _G\)).

  • PeterWeyl.convOp_bump_tendsto_id

Let \(\{ U_n\} \) be a countable neighbourhood basis at \(1_G\) shrinking to \(\{ 1_G\} \), and \(\phi _n\) a bump on \(U_n\) for each \(n\). Then for every \(f \in L^2(G)\), \(T_{\phi _n} f \to f\) in \(L^2\) as \(n \to \infty \).

Proof ▶

Three steps.

Step 1. For continuous \(f \in C(G)\), by uniform continuity (compactness, Heine–Cantor) of \(f\), given \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\) pick a neighbourhood \(U \ni 1_G\) with \(|f(u^{-1} x) - f(x)| {\lt} \varepsilon \) for all \(u \in U, x \in G\). For \(n\) large enough \(\phi _n\) has support in \(U\), so \(|(T_{\phi _n} f)(x) - f(x)| = |\int (f(y^{-1}x) - f(x))\phi _n(y)\, d\mu _G(y)| \leq \varepsilon \). Hence \(\| T_{\phi _n} f - f\| _{L^\infty } \to 0\), and a fortiori \(\| T_{\phi _n} f - f\| _{L^2} \to 0\) since \(\| \cdot \| _{L^2} \leq \| \cdot \| _{L^\infty }\) (Theorem 13).

Step 2. For general \(f \in L^2(G)\) and \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\), pick \(f' \in C(G)\) with \(\| f - f'\| _{L^2} {\lt} \varepsilon /3\) (Theorem 12). By Step 1, pick \(N\) with \(\| T_{\phi _n}f' - f'\| _{L^2} {\lt} \varepsilon /3\) for \(n \geq N\).

Step 3. Triangle inequality:

\[ \| T_{\phi _n}f - f\| _{L^2} \leq \| T_{\phi _n}(f - f')\| _{L^2} + \| T_{\phi _n}f' - f'\| _{L^2} + \| f' - f\| _{L^2}. \]

The middle term is \({\lt} \varepsilon /3\) by choice of \(N\). The first and third are each \({\lt} \varepsilon /3\) using \(\| T_{\phi _n}\| _{\mathrm{op}} \leq \| \phi _n\| _{L^2}\) (Definition 39)... but \(\| \phi _n\| _{L^2}\) is not bounded in general! We need a refined estimate: \(\| T_{\phi _n}(f - f')\| _{L^2} \leq \| f - f'\| _{L^2}\cdot \| \phi _n\| _{L^1} = \| f - f'\| _{L^2}\) using \(\| \phi _n\| _{L^1} = 1\) (the normalization condition) and Young’s inequality with \(L^2 \cdot L^1 \to L^2\). This bound is uniform in \(n\). Then the first term is \({\lt} \varepsilon /3\). Total: \({\lt} \varepsilon \).
